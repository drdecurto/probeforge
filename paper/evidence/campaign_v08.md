## Abstract

Small open language models answer multilingual quantitative problems inconsistently: the same problem phrased in Luxembourgish or Basque, or with a distractor sentence, yields a different answer than in English, and a problem with a missing fact is "solved" anyway. We present a verified contrast post-training pipeline (ProbeForge/ContrastForge) that generates parametric problem worlds in 13 languages, renders each world under eight views — a base statement, an answer-preserving paraphrase, a distractor, three metamorphic transformations (academic context, business context, expansion), a boundary view with a missing fact, and a counterfactual with perturbed facts — verifies every model response with an exact-rational checker, and trains LoRA adapters on *derivation-first* targets: a language-neutral formula trace emitted before the status and the answer. On a held-out benchmark of 12 unseen problem groups × 13 languages × 8 views (1,248 prompts), supervised fine-tuning on 3M tokens raises exact accuracy of Qwen3-8B from .036 to .318 ± .033 (3 seeds), of Qwen3-14B from .083 to .413 ± .005 and of Phi-4 from .022 to .341 ± .033 (3 seeds each), with per-language spread narrowing to ≈ .25–.45 and answer-preserving views within ±.02 of the base statement. Error-mined curricula and random curricula reach the same mean; the ordering is model-dependent. Adapters trained on five languages transfer to eight unseen languages without loss (.325 seen vs .311 unseen), and derivation-first adapters transfer to seven unseen problem families when the protocol leaves room for the derivation (.33 → .66–.70). Two evaluation-only confirmation packs of 6,240 prompts each confirm the gains with 60-group paired confidence intervals (8B +.183 [.125, .247]; 14B +.253 [.171, .333]) and show that the apparent counterfactual failure of earlier rounds was arithmetic difficulty rather than a lack of sensitivity: with number-class-preserving perturbations counterfactual accuracy equals or exceeds the base view. A status-diverse curriculum lifts boundary recognition from .80 to .99 at 14B at no mean cost but higher seed variance; off-policy and on-policy DPO add nothing; and mixed-mode targets show that the target style selects the inference regime of Qwen3: synthetic reasoning traces preserve the no-thinking regime but cap reasoning, while a small fraction of the base model's own verified traces yields the best reasoning-mode accuracy measured (.77 / .97 on the two subsets, 3 seeds) for a −.03 cost without reasoning. As a frontier reference, Muse-Spark-Open-816B-A42B scores .97 on the main benchmark and both confirmation packs with a 4,096-token budget on 16 GPUs, with its only weak language (Portuguese) explained by unit formatting rather than arithmetic. On standard benchmarks the adapters show no forgetting (Belebele and Global-MMLU-Lite within ±.01) and gain on MGSM (+.01 to +.05), and the derived 14B models lead six comparable open models including Gemma-3-12B, Llama-3.1-8B, Aya-Expanse-8B and EuroLLM-9B; on the independent 13-language reasoning benchmark of de Zarza et al. every derived model beats its base (Qwen3-8B .354 → .437 under the published protocol). Smaller models imitate the training format, which style-sensitive metrics penalise; mixing prose targets and verified multilingual GSM8K solutions from a concise teacher repairs this (3 seeds: Qwen3-8B .354 → .514 semantic and .306 → .690 answer accuracy on that benchmark, above the best published model; Qwen3-14B answer accuracy .450 → .859 under chat; Phi-4-mini answer accuracy +.064 [.002, .128] under chat, its semantic score unchanged within noise). All training ran in two-hour debug-queue jobs on a single 4×H100 node.

Research questions addressed by the pipeline:

- **RQ1 — Cross-lingual consistency.** Does verified contrast training make a small model answer the same problem equally well in 13 languages, including low-resource Luxembourgish and Basque?
- **RQ2 — Invariance versus sensitivity.** Does the model stay invariant under answer-preserving views (paraphrase, distractor, metamorphic context and expansion) while reacting correctly to boundary (missing fact) and counterfactual (changed fact) views?
- **RQ3 — Curriculum.** Does error-mined contrast beat random contrast at equal token budget, and does a preference stage (DPO) add anything on top of SFT?
- **RQ4 — Generalisation.** Do the learned behaviours transfer to unseen problem families and unseen languages?
- **RQ5 — Cost, scale and regime.** How do gains depend on model size and family (Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-14B, Phi-4), and how do no-thinking adapters relate to the base model's reasoning mode in accuracy and tokens?

## 2. Method

### 2.1 Problem worlds and views
Twelve parametric problem families (rate, affine, weighted mean, variance, constrained product, energy, work rate, rectangle, linear system, journey, integer production planning, compound interest) are instantiated with exact rational parameters. Each world is rendered in 13 languages (en es ca ar lb eu fr pt it de ru zh-cn zh-tw) from per-language templates, under eight views: `base`, `paraphrase`, `distractor` (an irrelevant sentence), `context_academic`, `context_business`, `expansion` (metamorphic relations: context injection and expansion), `boundary` (one required fact removed → `needs_clarification`), `counterfactual` (a fact perturbed → different answer). A later *status-diverse* variant (v3) adds `boundary_alt` (a second missing fact) and `infeasible` views for five families, moving the status distribution from 89 % `solved` to 75/17/7/1 % (solved / needs_clarification / infeasible / unbounded).

### 2.2 Verification
The model must answer with a JSON object `{status, answer, facts, explanation}` in any key order; the verifier checks `status` and the exact rational `answer` (and extracted facts) against the solver; `explanation` is ignored. Responses that do not stop within the 512-token budget count as wrong.

### 2.3 Derivation-first targets
Training completions are *worked* targets: `facts → explanation → status → answer`, where the explanation starts with a language-neutral formula trace (e.g. `speed_kmh = distance_km/duration_h = 6051/37 = 6051/37`) produced by a symbolic deriver, followed by a short authored sentence in the prompt language. The ordering lets an autoregressive model compute before it commits. Gold (answer-first) targets were used in round 1 and gave about half the gain.

### 2.4 Curricula and training
Base-model rollouts are collected on 7,748 mining prompts (60 train + 12 validation groups × 13 languages × views); every response is verified. Arms at an equal 3M-token budget: `translated` (base view only), `random` (uniform over views), `mined` (verified errors of the student weighted in), `no_counterfactual` (ablation). LoRA (r = 16, α = 32, all projections), bf16, batch 1 × 32 accumulation, lr 1e-4, one epoch, ≈ 60–76 min on one H100. Seeds: 3 for the main Qwen arms. Base models: Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-14B, Phi-4 (14.7B). All jobs ran on the `acc_debug` queue (≤ 2 h, one node, 4×H100 64 GB) with resumable collection and training.

### 2.5 Evaluation
Main benchmark: 12 held-out groups (families unseen during training) × 13 languages × 8 views = 1,248 prompts, greedy, no-thinking template, 512-token budget. Metrics: exact pass@1, worst-language accuracy, per-view and per-family accuracy, root-world bootstrap 95 % CI and paired group differences against the base model. Two evaluation-only confirmation packs (held-out families, 60 roots × 13 languages × 8 views = 6,240 prompts each): `core60` (frozen) and `diag60` (number-class-preserving counterfactuals, ties and binding constraints, 5–8-year horizons). A coverage pack `dev112` (7 new families, en/es, 1,824 prompts) tests new-family transfer under two protocols. Thinking-mode evaluation uses the Qwen3 reasoning template with a 4,096-token budget on fixed subsets (26 prompts of the main benchmark; 52 of diag60).

Findings. (i) Verified contrast SFT on 3M tokens lifts exact accuracy by an order of magnitude on every base model; paired differences against the base are positive with CIs excluding zero for every random/mined arm (8B: +.21 to +.32; 14B: +.27 to +.33; Phi-4: +.24 to +.35 over six seeds). (ii) Translated-only data (base view, no contrast) gives a third to a half of the gain and zero boundary recognition; removing the counterfactual view (8B `no_counterfactual`: .173, boundary .00) likewise destroys status recognition — the contrast views are necessary, not decorative. (iii) Mined vs random: a tie on mean at 8B (mined lower variance, better worst language), random ahead at 14B, and at Phi-4 a single-seed mined lead (.376 vs .305) that disappears with three seeds (random .341 ± .033, mined .317 ± .058) — the arm ordering is not robust for any model; the gain from verified contrast is (paired Δ vs base positive with CI excluding zero for every seed of every random/mined arm, Appendix C). (iv) Size: base 14B is only .083 — scale alone does not produce the behaviour; identical training adds +.05–.10 over 8B. (v) Completion length drops from 200–300 to ≈ 110–145 tokens after training.

### 3.5 Status-diverse curriculum (RQ2, RQ3)

![fig6](figures/fig6_v3_seeds.png)

*Figure 6. v2 vs v3 at 14B, 3 seeds each.*

Adding `boundary_alt` and `infeasible` views (v3) raises boundary recognition from .80–.84 to .98–.99 at 14B with the mean unchanged (v3 random .386 ± .061, v3 mined .389 ± .036 vs v2 .413 ± .005 / .365 ± .020) but with 3–10× larger seed variance; at 8B the same data cost .02–.07 on solved views (v3 random .325, boundary .96). On diag60 the v3 adapter improves boundary under the pack's alternate construction from .50 to .67 and worst-language from .32 to .41 (Δ +.022 [−.019, .061]). Status-diverse data buys robustness of status recognition, not mean accuracy, and should be reported with seeds.

### 3.6 Preference optimisation (RQ3)

Off-policy DPO (rejected = base model's verified errors, chosen = worked target) degrades the SFT adapter (8B: .353 → .292, Δ −.062 [−.119, −.008]). On-policy DPO (rejected = the SFT model's own verified errors) is a null result (+.002 [−.005, .010]): the SFT model solves 90 % of its own mining prompts, leaving 216 pairs. A preference stage would need harder held-in prompts or multi-sample rollouts to find errors at scale.

| adapter | no-think (1,248) | think26 (main) | think60 (diag60) | think tokens | no-think boundary |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| base14b | .083 | .62 | 1.00 | 1757 / 531 | .33 |

| distillsyn (distilled + synthetic to 50 %), 3 seeds | .365 ± .014 | .55 ± .02 | .92 ± .06 | ≈ 350 | .76 |

With reasoning on, the untrained 14B solves ≈ .62 of the main subset at 1.8k reasoning tokens and all of the integer-friendly diag60 subset; no-thinking adapters reach .41 at ≈ 140 tokens. Adapters trained only in the no-thinking regime shorten and degrade the reasoning regime on easy arithmetic (.44). Mixing in training rows rendered under the reasoning template decides the regime: with a *synthetic* derivation trace on half the rows (`mixed50`) the no-thinking regime is essentially preserved (−.013, consistent across seeds) and the easy-arithmetic reasoning regime recovers (.88), but reasoning is capped at ≈ 100 tokens and the hard-arithmetic subset stays at .50 for every seed — the model recites the formula instead of exploring. With the base model's *own verified traces* on the 12 % of rows where it succeeded (`distill`), the reasoning regime becomes the best measured (.77 on the main subset, above the untrained base .62 and the no-thinking-trained adapter .69; .97 on diag60) at base-like trace length, for a cost of −.026 ± .023 in the no-thinking regime. Combining distilled and synthetic traces (`distillsyn`) is the worst of both: the synthetic fill drags reasoning short (≈ 350 tokens) while the no-thinking regime loses −.048. Training only under the reasoning template (`think100`) damages the no-thinking regime (−.156). The target style selects the regime; where both regimes matter, a small fraction of distilled traces is the recipe, and synthetic traces should not be mixed with them.

### 3.8 Efficiency (RQ5)

Collection runs at ≈ 16 prompts/min/GPU (8B) and ≈ 13 (14B/Phi-4); one SFT arm of 3M tokens takes 60 min (8B), 72–76 min (14B), 59 min (Phi-4) on a single H100; a full round (collect 2 chunks + 3 arms + sharded eval) fits in ≈ 5.5 h of two-hour debug jobs. Trained no-thinking adapters answer in ≈ 1–2 s at ≈ 140 tokens versus 20–56 s and 0.5–1.8k reasoning tokens for the base model's thinking mode. *[TODO: energy from NVML where available.]*

### 3.9 Frontier reference: Muse-Spark-Open (RQ5)

To place the adapters against a current frontier open-weight model we evaluated Meta's Muse-Spark-Open-816B-A42B (NVFP4 release; byte-identical to the official checkpoint, 127/127 shard hashes) on every set used above. It was served with vLLM (Muse container build) on 16 H100 (4 nodes, tensor-parallel 4 × pipeline-parallel 4, Marlin W4A16 MoE kernels, since NVFP4 activations are not supported on Hopper). Muse always reasons in a separate channel; we use `reasoning_effort=low` with a 4,096-token budget as the analogue of the no-thinking regime and `xhigh` for the thinking subsets. Under the 512-token protocol of the adapters, 85 % of its answers are cut off inside the reasoning channel (.149), so the 4k protocol is reported as the reference.

| set | Muse-Spark (ref.) | best 14B adapter | 14B base |
|---|---|---|---|
| main benchmark (1,248) | .974 [.962, .986], worst pt .85, 697 tokens | .413 ± .005 (random, 3 seeds), 145 tokens | .083 |
| core60 (6,240) | .972 [.966, .978], worst pt .87 | .323 [.248, .396] | .070 |
| diag60 (6,240) | .958 [.948, .968], worst pt .82 | .424 [.327, .524] (v3) | – |
| think26 / think60 (xhigh) | 1.00 (2,114 tokens) / .96 (1,555) | .77 / .97 (distill, 3 seeds) | .62 / 1.00 |
| dev112 (7 unseen families, native protocol, lenient) | .991 | .66–.70 (8B) | .33 (8B) |

The frontier model is near the ceiling of the verifier on every set, including boundary (.89–.96) and counterfactual (.96–.98) views; the remaining gap of the 14B adapters is therefore capability, not benchmark noise. Two caveats matter for comparison. (i) Cost: Muse spends ≈ 700 tokens (of which ≈ 520 reasoning) per answer on 16 GPUs, against ≈ 145 tokens on one GPU for the adapters. (ii) Format: Muse's weakest language is Portuguese on every set (.82–.87), and inspection shows the failures are formatting, not arithmetic — in Portuguese it attaches units to the exact rationals ("580/7 km/h") or returns `{value, unit}` objects. A unit-stripping sensitivity rescore (applied to all languages) raises core60 to .988 (Portuguese .98, worst language Catalan .94) and diag60 to .980; we report the strict numbers as primary. On `dev112` its strict two-key score is 0 for the same schema reason as the adapters (it keeps the four-key format). Applied to the 14B thinking subsets, the distilled-trace adapter (.77 / .97) closes most of the gap to Muse (1.00 / .96) at comparable reasoning length on one GPU.

### 3.10 External multilingual reasoning benchmark (de Zarza et al., 2026)

![fig7](figures/fig7_energy_benchmark.png)

*Figure 7. 13-language multi-step reasoning benchmark of de Zarza et al. (Electronics 15(7):1395) under its published protocol; grey bars are the published results, the dark bar our re-run of the published Phi-4-mini (`tables/table7_energy_benchmark.csv`).*

To test whether verified-contrast training transfers to an independent benchmark, we re-ran the 13-language reasoning benchmark of de Zarza et al. (79 problems per language in six categories, three runs per problem, score = mean of MiniLM semantic similarity and numeric overlap against the reference solution, NVML energy at batch 1) on every derived model and its base. The published protocol (raw completion of "Solve this problem step by step: …", 300 tokens, temperature 0.2) is reproduced exactly: our re-run of Phi-4-mini-instruct scores .505 against the published .507.

| model | base | derived (best arm) | Δ | published comparators |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Qwen3-8B | .354 | .437 (mined); v3 random .428 | +.083 | Phi-4-mini .507 |
| Qwen3-14B | .359 | .426 (mixed50); v3 mined .382, mined .377, random .376, distill .366 | +.067 | Phi-3-mini .476, Qwen2-7B .467 |
| Phi-4 | *(empty completions)* | *(empty completions)* | – | Falcon-Mamba .384, Mistral-7B .332 |

Every adapter scores at or above its base in the 13-language mean; the largest gains are in English, French, Russian and Traditional Chinese (8B mined: ru .34 → .47, zh-tw .38 → .52) and the smallest in Basque and Luxembourgish. Under the raw protocol the absolute level of Qwen3 is depressed by its hybrid-reasoning habit of continuing the prompt with an English "Okay, so …" monologue inside the 300-token budget, and Phi-4 (14B) emits end-of-text immediately (zero-length completions), so its raw scores are not interpretable.

![fig8](figures/fig8_energy_chat.png)

*Figure 8. Same benchmark with the prompt wrapped in each model's chat template (no thinking); dashed line: Phi-4-mini under the same protocol.*

We therefore also report a chat-template variant (identical problems, scoring and budget; the prompt is wrapped in the model's own chat template with thinking disabled). Phi-4-mini moves only from .505 to .513, so the variant is calibrated against the published scale. Under it every derived model again beats its base: Phi-4 .424 → .446, Qwen3-14B .446 → .482 and Qwen3-8B .445 → **.523**, above the published best (Phi-4-mini). The derived models also stop earlier and draw less energy per 1,000 queries (Qwen3-8B 845 → 417 Wh; Phi-4 1,555 → 317 Wh). One sub-metric moves the other way: the step-accuracy score, which matches reference step phrasings, is lower for derived models (.59 vs .66 for 8B) because derivation-first answers are compact formula traces rather than prose steps. The gains on this independent benchmark — different problem types, free-form answers, semantic rather than exact scoring — suggest that verified-contrast training improves multilingual quantitative reasoning beyond the trained JSON task; because the benchmark's score is semantic rather than exact, the standard benchmarks of section 3.11 are the stricter test.

| EuroLLM-9B-Instruct | .698 | .593 | .544 |
| Salamandra-7B-instruct | .597 | .512 | .220 |

Three findings. (i) **No forgetting.** On reading comprehension and knowledge the Qwen3 adapters stay within ±.01 of their base (Belebele .889–.893 vs .892; Global-MMLU-Lite .718–.722 vs .729), and the Phi-4 adapter improves both (+.018, +.033). (ii) **Transfer to standard maths.** Every adapter improves MGSM over its base: +.014 to +.026 at 14B (to .883), +.039 to +.050 at 8B and +.038 for Phi-4 (1,500 items per model) — training on synthetic, verified, exact-rational problems in 13 languages carries over to free-form multilingual word problems in a format the adapters never saw. (iii) **Position among comparable models.** The derived 14B models are the strongest of the 18 evaluated models on all three benchmarks except Global-MMLU-Lite, where the untrained Qwen3-14B base leads by ≤ .01; they lead the strongest comparator (Gemma-3-12B-it) by .005–.009 on Belebele, .005–.009 on Global-MMLU-Lite and .055–.067 on MGSM. The derived 8B and Phi-4 models are above every 7–9B comparator (Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3.1-8B, Aya-Expanse-8B, EuroLLM-9B, Salamandra-7B) on all three. Most of the lead over the comparators comes from the Qwen3/Phi-4 bases themselves; what training adds is the MGSM gain at no cost elsewhere. The mixed-mode adapter tends to keep generating after stating its answer in few-shot completion mode, which lowers the last-number ("flexible") MGSM score (.769) but not the strict score (.879); `table8` reports both.

### 3.12 The smallest model: Phi-4-mini, format lock-in and a mixed-format repair (v4)

Phi-4-mini-instruct (3.8B) is the strongest model of the published 13-language benchmark (.507), so we trained it with the identical pipeline (7,748 mining rollouts, 3M-token arms). On the verified task it learns, but by half as much as the larger models: base .050 → random .163 (Δ +.112 [.056, .219]) / mined .148 (Δ +.098 [.064, .145]); boundary recognition .37 → .81–.83. On the external benchmarks it does **not** improve: the published-protocol semantic score falls (.505 → .427 mined / .392 random), the chat-template score is flat (.513 → .514 / .521), and Belebele, Global-MMLU-Lite and MGSM drop slightly (MGSM .723 → .705).

The outputs show why. The adapter transfers the *style* of its training targets — a terse formula trace followed by a stock sentence ("El resultado se obtiene de…") — to every quantitative question, and the benchmark's score (semantic similarity to step-by-step reference solutions) penalises that style even when the answer is right. A stricter secondary score that only asks whether the run contains the reference key value (`table9`) separates the two effects:

| model | answer accuracy, raw: base → mined → v4 | answer accuracy, chat: base → mined → v4 |
|---|---|---|
| Phi-4-mini | .548 → .418 → **.574** | .576 → **.637** → .546 |
| Qwen3-8B | .306 → **.525** → .505 | .457 → **.746** → .561 |
| Qwen3-14B | .374 → .534 (mixed50) | .450 → .641 (random) |
| Phi-4 | – (empty raw completions) | .374 → .633 / .631 / .595 (3 seeds of mined) |

Under a chat prompt every original adapter makes its model *more often correct* on this independent benchmark (Phi-4-mini +.061, Qwen3-8B +.289, Qwen3-14B +.191, Phi-4 +.259) although the semantic score barely moves; under raw completion the smallest model loses because the terse style also leaks into continuation mode.

**v4: format diversity plus real problems.** To break the lock-in we trained a mixture at the same 3M-token budget: 50 % contrast JSON (the mined curriculum), 25 % solved contrast rows re-rendered as prose (plain step-by-step instruction, formula trace, native-language "The answer is N"), and 25 % real problems — GSM8K *training* items (no overlap with MGSM test) translated into the 12 other languages by Qwen3-14B, kept only when every number survives translation, with step-by-step solutions from Qwen3-14B kept only when the final answer equals the GSM8K gold (24,932 verified problem–language pairs; ≈ 190 per language used). v4 keeps the verified task (8B .294 vs .318 ± .033; Phi-4-mini .162 vs .148/.163), removes Phi-4-mini's raw-protocol loss (semantic .427 → .509, answer accuracy .418 → .574, both at or above base) and gives it its first MGSM gain (.723 → .755, +.032), and gives Qwen3-8B its best raw-protocol score (.458). It does not yet help under chat: v4 copied the teacher's LaTeX-heavy, longer solution style (40–80 % of outputs contain LaTeX vs ≈ 2 % for the mined adapters), so within the benchmark's 300-token budget it more often stops before the answer (8B chat answer accuracy .561 vs .746). **v4b: a concise teacher.** Regenerating the GSM8K solutions with a teacher-only instruction ("at most 6 short steps, plain text, no LaTeX or Markdown"; student prompt unchanged) and rejecting solutions over 250 tokens or with LaTeX removes the style problem (LaTeX in 2–7 % of outputs) and gives the best external results of the campaign:

| model | semantic raw / chat | answer acc. raw / chat | MGSM strict | verified task |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Qwen3-8B base | .354 / .445 | .306 / .457 | .805 | .036 |
| Qwen3-8B mined | .437 / .523 | .525 / .746 | .844 | .318 ± .033 |
| Qwen3-8B v4b | **.520 / .585** | **.717 / .834** | .816 | .252 |
| Phi-4-mini base | .505 / .513 | .548 / .576 | .723 | .050 |
| Phi-4-mini mined | .427 / .514 | .418 / .637 | .705 | .148 |

Paired cluster bootstrap over the 79 problems (13 languages each): for Qwen3-8B every gain is large and significant (semantic raw +.166 [.141, .192], chat +.140 [.105, .174]; answer accuracy raw +.411 [.345, .476], chat +.376 [.284, .469]; v4b − mined, chat semantic +.061 [.037, .087]), and Qwen3-8B v4b now exceeds the best published model (Phi-4-mini .507) on every measure of this benchmark. For Phi-4-mini, v4b makes the model significantly more often correct under the published protocol (answer accuracy +.051 [.009, .093]; under chat the mined adapter already gave +.061 [.009, .111]), while the semantic score moves within noise (raw +.019 [−.005, .043], chat −.030 [−.063, .000]). The repairs have costs: v4b gives up part of the JSON-task gain at 8B (.252 vs .318) and v4's MGSM gain for Phi-4-mini (.719 vs .755). Half of the Italian and Portuguese GSM8K items were lost because the teacher copied the instruction's placeholder literally ("La risposta è N."); with a placeholder-free instruction ("…termina con 'La respuesta es' seguido del número final") the yield rises to 2,705 (pt) and 2,691 (it) and the full set to 32,260 verified problem–language pairs.

Paired cluster bootstrap of the seed-averaged model against its base: Qwen3-8B semantic +.160 [.136, .186] raw and +.120 [.084, .155] chat, answer accuracy +.384 [.321, .445] and +.346 [.262, .431]; Qwen3-14B (3 seeds) chat semantic +.123 [.088, .157] and answer accuracy +.409 [.316, .499] (the best result of the campaign, .859), raw +.044 [.033, .056] and +.132 [.098, .167]; Phi-4-mini chat answer accuracy +.064 [.002, .128], while its semantic scores move within noise (raw +.008 [−.017, .032], chat −.023 [−.055, .007]).

The recipe does not transfer to every base model. Aya-Expanse-8B never produces a correct verified answer before training (0/1,248). We gave it the full pipeline on its *own* verified rollouts (7,748 mining prompts; base accuracy on the mining set .008) and also the v4c recipe on its own and on a Qwen-derived curriculum: every arm reaches .077–.080 on the verified task (paired Δ +.08 [.04, .11]), and in every arm *all* of it comes from boundary views (needs_clarification .62–.64; base view .00). Of the mined adapter's 1,148 errors, 725 have the correct status and correctly extracted facts but a wrong exact answer — genuine arithmetic errors (e.g. 16100/30 + 250/60 → "805/3" instead of 3245/6). On the external benchmark every Aya arm is significantly *below* base under the published protocol (semantic −.024 to −.069) and unchanged under chat; MGSM is unchanged for the mined adapter (.707) and significantly lower for the mixed-format arms (.675, .663); Belebele and Global-MMLU-Lite are unchanged. Verified contrast SFT therefore amplifies an exact-arithmetic capability the base model already has (Qwen3, Phi-4, and to a lesser degree Phi-4-mini) but cannot install one in 3M tokens; gains are base-model dependent (Qwen3 ≫ Phi-4 > Phi-4-mini > Aya).

So, for the best published model of this benchmark, our training makes Phi-4-mini more often correct but does not raise the published semantic score beyond noise (.512 vs .505 re-run / .507 published); for the larger Qwen3 models the same recipe raises every measure far above the best published model, with no loss on Belebele or Global-MMLU-Lite and a +.02 MGSM gain.

Taken together: verified contrast training improves exact multilingual reasoning on an independent benchmark for every model when correctness is measured directly, but the smaller the model the more it imitates the training *format*, which style-sensitive metrics punish. Mixing formats and real problems repairs most of this at no cost on the verified task; the style of the reference solutions used for the real problems matters.

## 4. Discussion

- Verified contrast with derivation-first targets is the lever; the curriculum arm (random vs mined) is secondary and model-dependent, so claims about error mining should be made per model and with seeds.
- Invariance under metamorphic views is obtained for free once the model derives before it commits; sensitivity (boundary/counterfactual) must be *taught* through contrast views, and its measurement depends on the arithmetic class of the perturbation.
- Protocol matters for derivation-first models: removing the explanation slot (two-key schema) collapses status recognition on unseen families from .66–.70 to .20.
- No-thinking SFT is not regime-neutral on Qwen3: it competes with the native reasoning mode; mixed-mode data can be tuned toward either regime but, so far, not both at once.
- A frontier open-weight model (816B total, 42B active) solves the same verified tasks at .96–.97 at ≈ 5× the tokens and 16× the GPUs; trained 14B adapters reach ≈ 40 % of that in the no-thinking regime and 77–100 % in the reasoning regime with distilled traces.
- Limitations: single seeds for `think100`, the v4/v4b development arms, the translated-only arms and the Aya arms; thinking subsets of 26/52 prompts; synthetic worlds (no real-world text); one LoRA configuration; held-out families drawn from the same generator as the training families, except `dev112`.
